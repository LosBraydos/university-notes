# Properties of Light
Light has wave-like properties, which affects how it interacts with objects and itself.
Properties of light:
- Wavelength
- Frequency
- Amplitude 
![[Pasted image 20260922111909.png]]
Wavelength and frequency are related and correspond to the energy of the light. A higher frequency leads to a shorter wavelength and corresponds to higher energy, while a low frequency and longer wavelength corresponds to lower energy. The visible spectrum of light is the range of wavelengths of light that our eyes can see and interpret as colour. Longer wavelength light (lower energy) is interpreted as toward the red end of the spectrum, while short wavelengths (higher energy) is interpreted as towards the blue end. The amplitude corresponds to the intensity or brightness of the light.
Light interacts with objects and itself through:
- Reflection
- Absorbance
- Transmittance
- Interference
When light bounces of an object, it is **reflected**. Different materials can **absorb** certain wavelengths ("colours") of light, and some can reemit the absorbed light at a lower frequency which is called **fluorescence**, or **phosphorescence** if it reemits the light over an extended period of time. Transmission occurs when light passes through a material. If the material is **transparent**, a large portion of light is **transmitted** through the material. Glass is one example.
When light waves interact with each other, they undergo constructive and/or destructive **interference**. 
As light travels through a transparent medium, it may also **refract**. The speed of the light relative to a vacuum is lower which leads to a change in direction. This is the basis for how lenses focus light. The degree that a material bends light compared to vacuum is called the material's **refractive index**.

As parallell beams of light pass through a lens, they are focused towards a single point called the **focal point**. The distance from the lens to the focal point is the **focal length**. 

# Light Microscopy
There are three things that determine the ability of a microscope to enlarge images of small objects:
- Magnification - The ability to enlarge the image of an object compared to the real object
- Resolution - The ability to distinguish two close objects as separate
- Numerical aperture - The lens' ability to gather light
Resolution is affected by the wavelength and the numerical aperture. A shorter wavelength is able to resolve a more detailed image. In some applications, even high frequency light has too long wavelength to effectively resolve certain details. For this, an **electron microscope** uses electrons instead of light due to the much shorter wavelengths of electrons.
