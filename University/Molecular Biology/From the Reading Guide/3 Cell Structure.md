All cells have:

- Plasma membrane
- Chromosomes

Size, shape, structure, and functions vary largely among species and families

# Prokaryotes
- Prokaryotes - "before" nucleus, lacks nucleus. May have nucleoid, an area with a higher concentration of chromosomes
- Eukaryotes - have a nucleus surrounded by a complex nuclear membrane

Bacterial cell wall:
Membrane and peptidoglycan layers. Entire structure surrounding the bacteria is called *cell envelope*. 
Peptidoglycan layer - major component of cell wall.
- Long chains of alternating N-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM)
- Gram-negative: Cross-linked directly via tetrapeptide chains extending from each NAM unit
- Gram-positive: Tetrapeptide chains are bridged by pentaglycine cross-bridges![[Pasted image 20261126114518.png|677]]
- Difference: Gram-negative tetrapeptides are directly linked to each other, gram-positive tetrapeptides are linked via an intermediate pentaglycine bridge

Gram positive:
- Thick PG layer, 30-100 nm
- Embedded in layer of teichoic acid mycolic acid
- Plasma membrane is surrounded only by PG layer, periplasmic space between

Gram negative:
- Thin PG layer, < 4 nm
- Plasma membrane forms the inner membrane. PG layer then outer membrane made of lipopolysaccharides (LPS), periplasmic space between each layer

Sporulation:
When conditions are unfavourable, the bacteria can form endospores. Endospores are dehydrated and highly resistant to extreme temperatures and radiation. No growth or metabolic activity. Once conditions are favourable again the spore grows into a bacterium.
Usually done by gram-positive bacteria.

Filamentous appendages:
- Fimbriae - short protein filaments on cell surface. Used for sticking to surfaces and number in hundreds
- Pili - longer protein appendages on cell surface. Fewer than fimbriae. F pilus acts as a bridge to transfer DNA between cells, a mechanism for spreading antibiotic resistance!
- Flagella - Long appendage that acts as a propeller, allowing the bacteria to swim in response to changes in environment. Different arrangement: one or several flagella depending on species. ![[Pasted image 20261126150535.png]]

Nucleoid:
Region of the prokaryotic cell where DNA and DNA-associated proteins are concentrated. Is not enclosed by a membrane like in eukaryotes. Prokaryotic chromosomes are typically circular and haploid. Nucleoid-associated proteins (NAPs) organise the genome in the nucleoid.

Prokaryotic ribosomes:
- Synthesises proteins
- Three structurally distinct domains
- Made of proteins and ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
- Floats freely in the cytoplasm
- A complete ribosome is 70S in size, made up of a small subunit (30S) and a large subunit (50S)

# Eukaryotes
Protozoans, algae, fungi, plants and animals

Nucleus surrounded by a complex nuclear membrane. Multiple linear chromosomes.
Organelles are bound to the membrane and organised by the cytoskeleton instead of free floating:
- Mitochondria
- Endoplasmic reticulum
- Golgi apparauts
- Lysosome and peroxisomes
