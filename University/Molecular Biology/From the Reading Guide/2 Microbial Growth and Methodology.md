# Bacterial Cell Division
- Asexual
- Binary fission
	- One cell splits into to daughter cells that are clones of the mother cell
	1. Cell grows larger
	2. DNA is replicated
	3. Formation of septum
	4. Cells separate

![[Pasted image 20261126110417.png|298]]
DNA replication: DNA is doubled
Cell elongation: cell length is increased prior to binary fission. PG is remodelled/expanded
FtsZ: Protein directing the final separation (cytokinesis) of the two cells. Forms a Z-ring at the septum and eventually ties off, completing cell division.


Doubling time or generation time is the time it takes for a population to double in size and is important for knowing how fast a strain of bacteria can grow. The doubling time for different species can vary from less than 20 minutes to more than 2 weeks.
A growth curve can be used to visualise the growth cycle of a *closed* culture in the laboratory.
The different phases of the curve shows the different stages of the population's growth.
![[Pasted image 20261005120425.png|536]]
Lag phase (1) shows when there is no increase in living bacterial cells. In this phase bacteria are repairing components and producing proteins required for growth. The log phase (2) shows when there is an excess of nutrients and the bacterial population may grow unhindered.  (3) Eventually as nutrients are consumed and decrease, some cells begin to die off and the population reaches the stationary phase. (4) When nutrients are too low to sustain the population more cells die than new cells are created and the population declines.

Measuring bacterial growth:
- Direct microscopic cell count
- Flow cytometry
- Live/dead stains
- Plate count (CFU)
- Extinction dilution
- Turbidity (OD)

Oxygen Requirements:
- Obligate aerobes - Requires oxygen to survive
- Obligate anaerobes - Can't survive in oxygen
- Facultative anaerobes - Able to use oxygen for respiration, but not required
- Aerotolerant anaerobes - Doesn't use oxygen for respiration, but survives in oxygen-rich environments.
- Microaerophiles - Requires oxygen, but at lower concentrations than atmospheric. Harmed by full atmospheric oxygen.

Reason: aerobic respiration may create reactive oxygen species (ROS) as a side product. Many bacteria have enzymes that neutralise ROS
- Superoxide dismutase (SOD)
- Peroxidase
- Catalase
Obligate anaerobes normally lack all three, aerotolerant anaerobes have SOD but no catalase.
Can test for catalase to classify bacteria.
