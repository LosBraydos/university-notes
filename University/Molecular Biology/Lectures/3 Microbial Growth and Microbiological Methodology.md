In prokaryotes the **generation/doubling time** is defined as the time it takes for the population to double
This varies depending on species and environment, <20 min to >2 weeks.

The Growth Curve:![[Pasted image 20260901091001.png]]

Given a closed culture in a laboratory, an environment with limited resources (oxygen, nutrients, etc.), the environment of the growth medium changes as the bacteria grows. As the environment changes the bacteria will change their gene expression to better adapt.
Many antibiotics only kill growing cells.

Lag phase:
- Cells grow in size, but not number.
- Make proteins needed for growth in medium
- Repair cell components

Log phase:
- Cells divided by binary fission, exponentially.
- Generation time genetically determined and depends on environmental conditions, **intrinsic growth rate**
- $N=N_0\cdot2^{^{n}}$
![[Pasted image 20260901091528.png]]

Stationary phase:
- Number of live cells reaches plateau, net growth is zero
- The number of cells dividing is equal to the number of cells dying
- Happens when nutrients and oxygen is depleted
- Waste products also accumulate
- Bacterial metabolism prioritizes survival
- Sporulating bacteria produces endospores

Death phase:
- Viable cells decrease
- New cells are fewer than cells dying
- Nutrients are fully depleted, and waste products accumulates to toxic levels
- Cell lysis
- Endospore formation

Significance:
- In clinical samples as sign of infection
- Quality control in pharmaceuticals, food, and water
- Standard method in all areas of microbiology research

How to measure growth:
- Direct microscopic count, methods to extrapolate population sizes
- Coulter counter, electrical current to detect bacterial particle
- Flow cytometry
- Total number of cells is measures, even dead ones
- Live/dead stains could be used to differentiate

- Plate count, measure colony forming units (cfu)
- Several cells can form one colony
- Often done in serial dilutions, due to there being many colonies (we want to measure a single colony)
- Sensitive to oxygen, bacteria is prepared separately and spread on plate
- Not sensitive to oxygen, bacteria can be poured on plate 

- Most probable number, extinction dilution
- Statistical procedure for estimating number of viable cells in diluted samples

- Turbidity, optical density
- Uses spectrophotometer to measure how "cloudy" the sample is, indirectly measures how much bacteria is present
- Some bacteria can produce biofilm or pigments, which may affect the results

Growth requirements:
- Depends on:
	- Oxygen
	- pH
	- Temperature
	- Osmotic pressure
	- Barometric pressure
	- Other organisms
- Most bacteria and archaea (~99 %) have never been cultured, their growth requirements are not known and are instead inferred through DNA sequencing if at all

Oxygen:
- Not all microorganisms require oxygen, some thrive in their absence
- Early life was anaerobic and is still common
**![[Pasted image 20260901094150.png]]
- Facultative prioritize oxygen, but can grow and survive without
- Aerotolerant tolerate oxygen, but don't use it
- Microaerophiles require a specific concentration or partial pressure of oxygen, somewhere in between aerobes and anaerobes
- Oxygen is toxic! Can spill electrons from the ETC, creating reactive oxygen species (ROS)
- Many bacteria have ROS detoxifying enzymes: superoxide dismutase, peroxidase, and catalase
- Obligate anaerobes usually lack all three enzymes. Aerotolerant have SOD, but no catalase
- Can be used to identify certain bacteria, like streptococci (cat-) from staphylococci (cat+)

pH:
- Concentration of H+ can affect structure of all macromolecules and cell processes
- Most bacteria are neutrophiles
- Some archaea can survive in pH 2

Temperature:
- Bacteria have been found in almost all temperature environments
- Molecular adaptations
- Each species have a limited range they can survive in

Osmotic and Barometric pressure, light:
- Most microbes have higher concentrations of solutes in cytoplasm than environment, creates osmotic stress
- Halophiles require high salt
- Halotolerant can grow in high salt environments, many common food-born illnesses are caused by these
- Barophiles are adapted to deep sea life, require high barometric pressures
- Photoautotrophs and photoheterotrophs need light of correct wavelengths

Bacteria adapted to survive a certain environment often needs that environment to survive
Much of food culture involves changing the environment to kill microbes and prevent growth

Growth media:
- Enriched (not same as enrichment culture), specific growth factors/ingredients for picky bacteria
- Complex, give lots of different nutrients
- Defined, controlled conditions
