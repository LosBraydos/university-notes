- Differences and similarities between prokaryotes and eukaryotes
- Bacterial cell structure, morphology
- Cell wall composition and function
- Cell membrane composition and function
- Function and structure of flagella, pili, fimbriae

Cells come in many shapes and sizes
All cells have:
- Cytoplasm
- Plasma membrane
- Chromosomes
- Ribosomes

Prokaryotes, before nucleus: Archaea, bacteria - lacks nucleus
Eukaryotes: Eukarya - nucleus surrounded by complex nuclear membrane

The Bacterial Cell Wall:
- Major component of cell wall is peptidoglycan (or murein)
	- Layer of long chains of alternativ molecules of N-acetylglocsamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM)
	- Gram-neg: tetrapeptide chains extending from each NAM unit are directly cross-linked
	- Gram-pos: tetrapeptide chains are linked by pentaglycine cross-bridges 
- Gram positive:
	- PG 30-100 nm
	- Embedded with teichoic acid, exclusive to gram positive bacteria
	- Acid-fast also mycolic acid layer
- Gram negative:
	- PG < 4 nm
	- Periplasmic space between the cell wall and the plasma membrane
	- Outer membrane (contains lipopolysaccharide (LPS) endotoxin)

In response to unfavourable conditions some bacteria can form endospores, **sporulation**
Generally happens similar to regular cell division, but asymmtrically
Several membranes are formed around the replicated DNA. A protective cortex is then formed around this spore and protein coat forms around that. The spore is then released

Fimbriae:
- Short appendage on cell surface
- Hundreds
- Attachment

Pili:
- Longer appendage on cell surface
- Fewer than fimbriae
- F-pili can transfer DNA between cells

Flagella:
- Allow bacteria to move in an aqueous environment
- Works like a propeller, literally rotates
- Different bacteria have different arrangements
- Can move in response to environment

Nucleoid:
While there is no nucleus, prokaryotic DNA and DNA-associated proteins are concentrated in a region called the nucleoid
- Genome is usually circular
- Haploid
- Not bound by a complex nuclear membrane
- NAPs organize


Eukaryotic cell structure:
Protozoans, algae, fungi, plants, and animals
- A nucleus surrounded by complex nuclear membrane
- Membrane-bound organelles in cytoplasm:
	- Mitochondria
	- Endoplasmic reticulum
	- Golgi apparatus
	- lysosomes and peroxisomes
	- organised cytoskeleton
Cellular processes are compartmentalized

Nucleus:
- two lipid bilayers form the complex nuclear membrane
- Membrane contains nuclear pores which control transport
- Usually multiple linear chromosomes
- DNA is condensed and organized by histones
- Shaped by nuclear lamina

Nucleolus:
- The dark, dense area of the nucleus
- Synthesis or ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
- Start of ribosome assembly, rest is completed in cytoplasm

Eukaryotes have two kinds of ribosomes:
- 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts (are very similar to bacterial!)
- 80S in cytoplasm and attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum

Endoplasmic reticulum:
System of tubules and cisternae with a single lipid bilayer
- Spaces inside cisternae are called lumen
- Two types:
	- Rough ER: attached ribosomes make membrane proteins
	- Smooth ER: biosynthesis of lipids, carbohydrate metabolism, break down toxic compounds.

Golgi apparatus:
- Membranous disks, dictyosomes, of single lipid bilayers

Lysosomes and peroxisomes


Cytoskeleton:
Structural support, transport within the cell, anchoring of organelles
- Microfilaments: Cell shape and movement
- Intermedia filaments: Mechanical stability
- Microtubules: Highways for moving organelles and vesicles, also form the mitotic spindle during division

Mitochondria:
Powerhouse of the cell
- Aerobic cellular respiration, produces lots of ATP
- Mitochondrial genome appears bacterial, endosymbiotic theory
- Two lipid membranes

Chloroplasts:
Photosynthesis in plants and algae
- Three membranes: outer, inner, and thylakoid
- Genome also appears to be bacterial

Plasma membrane and transport:
Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins
- Contains sterols that modifies fluidity, unlike prokaryotes
- Simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport used by both eukaryotes and prokaryotes
- Endocytosis only in eukaryotes
	- Phagocytosis
	- Pinocytosis
	- This is receptor-mediated

Flagella and cilia (microtubules):
- Some eukaryotes use flagella, but these are smaller and slower than prokaryotes
- Cilia are short and unique to eukaryotes
