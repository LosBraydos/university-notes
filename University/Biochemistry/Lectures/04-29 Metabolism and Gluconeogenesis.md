Important principles:
1. Metabolic processes are organized in pathways, series of linked reactions
2. ATP is energy currency. Generated by oxidation of fuels and consumed to power reactions
3. Reoccurring intermediates are common in many pathways
4. Metabolic pathways are highly regulated

Metabolic pathways are mapped by intermediates linked by enzymatic reactions![[Pasted image 20260429100638.png|462]]

Metabolism has energy-yielding and energy-requiring reactions
- Catabolism: degradative phase of metabolism - releases energy
- Anabolism (biosynthesis): the building phase of metabolism - requires energy (e.g. ATP)
![[Pasted image 20260429100733.png]]

Even if a reaction at standard conditions isn't spontaneous, by controlling the concentration of reactants a cell can make unfavorable reactions favorable.

Reactions are coupled to hydrolysis of ATP, making them thermodynamically stable.

Why is ATP hydrolysis favorable?
![[Pasted image 20260429101915.png|381]]
- Charge repulsion between phosphates. High density of negative charge. Cleavage relieves this.
- Improved hydration.
- Resonance stabilization of Pi

ATP is thermodynamically unstable. However, uncatalyzed hydrolysis of ATP is slow!
The activation energy is very high, kinetically stable.
Cells beneficially exploit reactions by controlling the kinetics of already thermodynamically favorable reactions.

In cells ATP does not provide energy by simple hydrolysis. Group transfers are done instead.
1. Transfer of Pi from ATP to substrate, activating it
2. Displacement of leaving group, Pi is an excellent leaving group

Compounds with high potential to transfer Pi have high phosphoryl-transfer potential

Glycolysis
- An almost universal central pathway to generate energy from glucose metabolism
- Ancient pathway, not even oxygen is required!
- Glucose (C6) is degrade to yield two pyruvate (C3) molecules
- 10 enzymatic steps
- Takes place in cytoplasm
- Overall equation:![[Pasted image 20260429103047.png]]
- Phosphate transfers central to the pathway!
- 2 ATP are consumed to yield 4 ATP


Redox in biochemistry
Carbon atoms exist in different oxidation states, depending on the elements with which they share electrons
Oxidation can be simplified as the carbon with more oxygen character being the more oxidized.

Dehydrogenases
Catalyze redox reactions in which NAD+ is the electron acceptor
- NAD+ is a biological electron carrier, sometimes exists as NADP+
- Important co-factor for enzymes catalysing *catabolic* oxidations
- NADP+ is almost always involved in *anabolic* reactions
The nicotinamide ring functions as a electron sink, able to accommodate a hydride ion


Sources of glucose
- Stored in polysaccharide and dietary carbohydrates feed into glycolysis
- Conversion of deitary carbohydrates to glucose
- Glycogen: polysaccharide form of glucose in animals

What happens after glycolysis?
- NAD+ must be regenerated to keep glycolysis going
- Under aerobic conditions:
	- Pyruvate is only intermediate in complete oxidation of glucose to CO2
	- NAD+ is regenerated by donating electron to O2
- Under anaerobic conditions:
	- Fermentation
	- A general term for processes that extract ATP but do not consume oxygen or change concentrations
	- NADH transfers electrons to pyruvate

Ethanol fermentation:
- Yeast and other microorganisms regenerate NAD+ by reducing pyruvate to ethanol and CO2

Lactic acid fermentation:
- In animal tissues (muscle) when oxygen is insufficient
- Reduction of pyruvate by NADH
- Cori Cycle in extreme exercise:
	- Glycolysis in muscle
	- Lactate from muscle transported via blood to liver

Gluconeogenesis and Regulation
- Synthesis of glucose from precursors such as lactate and pyruvate
- Important when glucose store (glycogen) is insufficient
- E.g. brain relies on glucose as major fuel source
- takes place primarily in liver

Principle:
- 7 of the reactions of glycolysis are reversible with deltaG close to 0
- Only 3 are irreversible and need "bypass" reactions
	1. Pyruvate + ATP+ GTP + HCO3(-) --> PEP + ADP + GDP + Pi + CO2
	2. fructose 1,6-bisphosphate + H2O --> fructose 6-phosphate + Pi
	3. glucose 6-phosphate + H2O --> glucose + Pi
