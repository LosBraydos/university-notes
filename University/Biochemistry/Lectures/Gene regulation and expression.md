Same genes can express different proteins during development
All cells have the same set of genes, but the genes are expressed differently depending on the cell

Regulation:
- Transcription (mRNA synthesis)
- Posttranslational modification of mRNA
- Degradation of mRNA
- Protein synthesis (translation)
- Post-translational modifications of proteins
- Protein targeting and transport

Expression (How, which and when?):
- Genes are transcribed and translated when they are needed
- *Housekeeping* genes code for products that are always needed
- Other genes are expressed when needed
- *Transcriptome*: all mRNA in a cell, adapts to changing conditions
- *Proteome*: all proteins in a cell --"--
- *Differentiation*: inactivationon of genes not needed
	- Applies to unicellular and especially multicellular organisms (cell types, changes)
- *Development*: changes in expression over time

Bacterial repressors:
- Repressors: proteins that block RNA polymerase binding/movement on DNA
- Operators: binding sites on DNA that bind repressors, generally located near a promoter
- Effector: a small molecule or a protein that regulates repressor binding to alter transcription. Binds to repressor and triggering a conformational change

Bacterial activators:
- Activator: enhance RNA polymerase-promoter interaction
- Activator binding sites are often adjacent to promoters that are bound weakly to or not at all by RNA polymerase alone
