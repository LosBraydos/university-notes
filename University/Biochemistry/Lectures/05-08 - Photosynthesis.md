Most important biochemical process in the biosphere

Plant cells:
- Similar to animal cells, but with additional parts
- Possess chloroplast, cell walls (cellulose), vacuole ("water bladder", gives turgor)

Chloroplasts:
- Site of light-driven electron flow and photosynthesis
- Stroma
- Thylakoids
- Granal thylakoids

Tightly organized light-harvesting complexes to absorb sunlight and capture energy in chemical form
Separation of positive and negative charge leading to electron flow

Photophosphorylation - ATP synthesis driven by light
- Electron flow through membrane carriers coupled to proton pumping
- H2O is the electron donor
- NADPH is formed (acceptor)

Memory rule:
- Catabolic - NADH
- Anabolic - NADPH
Holds true in most cases

Oxidative phosphorylation

Substrate level phosphorylation
- Glycolysis
- Citric acid cycle (ATP, GTP in yeast/textbooks)

ATP and NADPH produced in photosynthesis is used to reduce CO2 to form triose phosphate through Calvin cycle

Electron flow
- Cytochromes
- Quinones
- Iron-sulphur protein
Coupled to proton pumping that drives ATP synthase

Electromagnetic radiation
- Excited and ground states
- Quantum - quantity of energy in photon equal to the energy of electron jumping to higher energy level
- Excited state - electron in raised energy state, energetic and unstable
- Ground state - stable energy state of electron, when electron returns from excited state molecule fluoresces
- Fluorescence always at lower energy (longer wavelength) than absorbed light
- Emitted light is absorbed again by another molecule and sent out again: exciton-exciton transfer
- This is what happens in chlorophyll

Accessory pigments in plants
- Secondary light-absorbing pigments - carotenoids
- May be yellow, red, orange

Cyanobacteria and algae use other accessory pigments

Photochemical reaction centers
- Absorbed light is funnelled to reaction center
- An electron is removed, leading to an electron being taken from water instead
