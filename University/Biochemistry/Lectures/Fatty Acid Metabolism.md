Aerobic ATP yield from one glucose: 30 or 32
Fatty acids can provide even more energy

Outline:
1. Catabolism of fatty acids (beta oxidation)
2. Special cases
3. Fatty acid synthesis (anabolism)

Average 70 kg male:
- 11 kg of fat
- Can produce 420 000 kJ
- Same amount of energy in the form of glucose, would be 64 kg heavier

Fatty acids are stored as triacylglycerols.
As fatty acids are less oxidized than carbohydrates (less oxygens bound to carbons), more energy can be produced
Fatty acids are carboxylic acids with long fatty chains

Cells obtain fatty acid fuels from four sources:
1. Fats consumed in the diet
2. Fats stored in cells in special organelles, lipid droplets
3. Fats synthesized in one organ for export to another
4. Fats obtained by autophagy

Hormones trigger mobilization of stored triacylglycerols in lipid droplets
- Lipid droplets built up by monolayer of phospholipids
- Low blood glucose (starvation, fasting)
	- Glucagon and epinephrine released
	- Activates lipases that split FAs from glycerol
	- Release of glycerol and FA from lipid droplets as free fatty acids (FFAs)
- Serum albumin transports FAs in blood to cells that require it

Lipases:
Different types and specificities. Catalyses hydrolysis of fatty acids from glycerol

The glycerol backbone can also be degraded for energy. It is converted to dihydroxyacetone which can enter glycolysis directly

FA degradation:
First step: activation of FA
- Fatty acid is converted into a fatty acyl-CoA, catalysed by acyl-CoA synthetase (costs 1 ATP)
- Adenylyl transfer happens instead of phosphoryl transfer as it is better at driving unfavourable reactions

Second step: shuttle fatty acyl-CoA into mitochondria
- FaCoA can't cross mitochondrial membrane and is shuttled through via carnitine instead.

Beta oxidation of FA:
- Takes place in mito. matrix
- Oxidation takes place at beta position of FA
- Stepwise removal of 2-carbon units in the form of acetyl-CoA
- Large amount of electrons are generated from beta oxidation and CAC done on resulting acetyl-CoA.

For an even number FA:
beta oxidation is done (n/2 - 1) times --> yields (n/2) acetyl CoA, (n/2 - 1) FADH2, (n/2 - 1) NADH, (n/2 - 1) H+

Palmitate
For 8 acetyl CoA: 24 NADH, 8 FADH2, 8 ATP
2.5 ATP for every NADH
1.5 ATP for every FADH2

Total 108 ATP from palmitate

Special cases:
1. Monounsaturated FA
	- Cannot introduce double bond between alpha and beta if there is a double bond between beta and gamma
	- Enzyme enoyl-CoA isomerase converts it into the alpha-beta alkene form
2. Polyunsaturated FA
	- Dienoyl is not a substrate for next beta oxidation enzyme
	- Dienoyl-CoA-reductase reduces one of the double bonds and enoyl-CoA isomerase moves the other double bond to the correct position
3. Odd-numbered FA
	- Last step forms a C3 body: propionyl-CoA
	- Can't be processed in beta oxidation
	- Solution:
		1. Carboxylase with cofactor biotin adds carboxyl to beta carbon to form C4 body
		2. Intramolecular rearrangement via B12-depented mutase, forms succinyl-CoA which can be use in CAC

FA Synthesis:
FA synthesis uses different enzymes than FA catabolism (beta oxidation)
Takes place in cytoplasm (not mitochondria)
Common principle: anabolic pathways characterized by reductions
In mammals, there is a single polypeptide chain catalysing FA synthesis reactions (FA synthase)
Intermediates of FA synthesis stay attached to ACP (acyl-carrier protein)
Uses NADP+ instead of NAD+ (NADP+ electron carrier like NAD+, but used in anabolic pathways whereas NAD+ is used in catabolic pathways)

FA synthesis starts with formation of malonyl-CoA
- Catalysed by acetyl CoA carboxylase + biotin coenzyme
- ATP-dependent, makes substrate more reactive
