Most energy stored in animals is fat
Fatty acids do not cross blood-brain barrier, not a good energy source for energy hungry brain!
Muscle also needs quick access to energy (glucose)
Glucose can be made rapidly available from glycogen, stored in liver and muscle cells

Glycogen:
- Branched polymer of glucose
- Forms granules
- Granules present in well-fed animals, starving animals do not possess granules (depleted)
- 50 000 glucose molecules!
- 2 000 termini on which enzymes can act
- Protein dimer in centre: glycogenin

Glycogenolysis:
- Glycogen phosphorylase cleaves off glucose (phosphorylisis). Adds Pi group.
- Keeps going until four glucoses away from branching point
- Glycogen debranching enzyme takes over
	- Transfer branch to form straight chain, leaving one on branch
	- Chops off final glucose

Glycogen phosphorylase makes glucose-1P, but glucose-6P is needed for glycolysis:
- Phosphoglucomutase converts to the correct glucose

Glucose-6P is converted into glucose by glucose-6-phosphatase in the ER of liver cells. Glucose is shuttled into blood stream and goes to the brain. Happens in ER so it is removed from glycolysis (compartmentalised, allows separate regulation)

Glycogen synthesis:
- Done from UDP-glucose (uridine diphosphate)
	- Activated (UDP is good LG)
	- UDP "tags" glucose for glycogen synthesis, separating it from other processes
- Glycogenin acts as primer, glucose gets attached
- ...
- ...
- Glycogen synthase takes over after primer. Extends further with alpha-1,4 linkages
- Branching enzyme forms alpha-1,6 branches

Regulation of synthesis and breakdown:
- Glycogen phosphorylase is regulated by phosphorylation
- Hormones glucagon (liver) and epinephrine (muscle) trigger phosphorylation
- Activates glycogen phosphorylase and thereby breakdown of glycogen (mechanism in Biochemical Signaling lecture)
- Glucose can act as inhibitory regulator by binding to glycogen phosphorylase (too much glucose, inhibit glucose production)
- Promotes dephosphorylation and inactivation of enzyme
- Insulin also promotes dephosphorylation, secreted at high blood glucose levels


Nitrogen gas makes up 78 % of atmosphere and is important for AA, DNA. But N2 is very unreactive (inert) due to strong triple bond.
NH3 (fixed nitrogen) is a source for biosynthesis
Nitrogen fixation involves reducing N2 to NH3 and is done by diazotrophs

Nitrogen-fixing nodules:
- Symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria invade roots of legumes
- Forms root nodules where they fix nitrogen
- Bacteria get carbohydrates and CAC compounds from plants
- Plants get fixed nitrogen

Nitrogenase complex:
- Nitrogen fixation is energetically costly
- Stepwise transfer of electrons onto nitrogen
- Details of the mechanism and energetics are still being researched

Assimilation of ammonia:
- Glutamine synthetase is entry point for incorporation of NH4+ into biomolecules. Glu converts to Gln
- Glu works as "landing platform" for new ammonia. Gln transfers to other molecules
- Highly regulated enzyme with central metabolic role
- In animals: glutamate comes from AA catabolism

Biosynthesis of AA:
- Carbon skeleton from:
	- CAC
	- Glycolysis
	- Pentosephosphate pathway
- Most organisms can make all 20 AA (chad)
- Humans cannot make 9 of these (W T F V H M I L K)

Dietary protein can be oxidized for energy:
- For carnivores, most energy comes from here
- For herbivores, only minor contribution
- Humans, only 10-15 %
	- Protein is last resort, when all other energy stores are exhausted

Digestion:
- Begins in stomach, low pH denatures proteins into random coils
- Pepsin then degrades proteins into peptides. Pepsin is a nonspecific protease most active at pH 2
- Pancreas secretes NaHCO3 to neutralise and zymogens, inactivated proteases
- Zymogens are then activated in the intestine into active forms trypsin and chymotrypsin (protects against self-digestion, zymogen is very aggressive)
- Trypsin + chymotrypsin digest peptides into free AA
- Later transported to liver, which is the main site for AA catabolism. Amino groups and carbon skeleton are metabolized separately
- Ammonia gets produced

Ammonia is toxic and needs to be removed or converted:
- Fish directly excrete ammonia into surrounding water (dilution)
- Terrestrial animals excrete nitrogen as urea
- Birds and reptiles excrete uric acid (different from urea!)
