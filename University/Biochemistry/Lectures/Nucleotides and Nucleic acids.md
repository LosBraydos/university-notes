Central Dogma: DNA to RNA to Protein, information flows in one direction

Nucleotides: monomeric building blocks of DNA and RNA
- Built up from a sugar (ribose), a nitrogen containing base, and phosphates
- If the ribose has a hydroxyl on 2' position, it is RNA. If no hydroxyl on 2' position it is dehydroxyribose (DNA)
- Nucleoside refers only to sugar + base, while nucleotide includes the phosphates as well

The bases of DNA and RNA:
- Purines: A, G - two fused rings (5 + 6) 
- Pyrimidines: C, T or U - single ring (6)

Nucleotides are linked together by phosphodiester linkages, covalent bonds between the 5'-phosphate and the 3'-hydroxyl group of the next nucleotide. This forms a sugar-phosphate backbone forming a strand of DNA or RNA.

Hierarchical levels of NA structure
- Primary - covalent structure and nucleotide sequence
- Secondary - regular, stable structure taken up by some or all the nucleotides
- Tertiary - complex folding of large chromosomes or the elaborate folding of tRNA or rRNA structure

DNA double helix:
- Anti-parallell: both strands go in opposite directions
- Base pairs: Purine + pyrimidine
	- A+T (U): two H-bonds
	- G+C: three H-bonds
- Major and minor grooves are formed on the helix, major grooves being more accessible for enzymes by being a larger space

Double helix is stabilised by:
- Metal cations shielding negative charge of phosphate backbone
- Base stacking interactions between successive base pairs
	- CG or GC (triple H-bonds) are stronger than AT/TA (double H-bonds)

Double helix exists in several forms:
- A, B, and Z
- B-DNA is considered "normal" or most common, but variations can occur due to syn and anti conformations of nucleoside and if the sugar is C2'-endo or C3'-endo
- A- and B-DNA are right-handed, Z-DNA is left-handed. They go from broadest to narrowest in A, B, and Z
