#### Lipids
- Diverse group of chemicals, class of related molecules
- Water insoluble hydrocarbons
- Very energy rich, more than carbs
- Stored as triacylglycerols
- Components of biological membranes, but also has additional cell functions (e.g. signalling)

Fatty acids are core constituents of lipids
- Fatty acids are carboxylic acids with hydrocarbon tails from 4 to 36 carbons long
- Can feature double bonds
- Notations: chain length, number of double bond and positions of double bonds![[Pasted image 20260424100657.png]]
- Fatty acids often have common names, historical reasons but normally not used. Prefer systematic names.
- Common patterns:
	- Most common is even number of carbon atoms in an unbranched chain 12 to 24 long
	- Monounsaturated, double bond usually on C-9 or C-10
	- Polyunsaturated, double bonds are usually on C-12 and C-15 and separated by a methylene group
	- Normally in cis-config.
- Physical properties:
	- Poor solubility in water, longer chain lower solubility
	- Carboxylic acid group is polar and ionized at neutral pH
	- At room temperature saturated fatty acids are waxy while unsaturated are oily liquids due to steric hindrance reducing packing.

Triacylglycerols
- Simplest lipid constructed from fatty acids
- Composed of three fatty acids bonds to a glycerol by ester bonds
- Can be simple, of one kind, or mixed, of two or three kinds of fatty acids
- non-polar, hydrophobic
- Provides storage and insulation

#### Biological membranes
- Composed of lipids and enable separation and compartmentalisation of the cell
- Control flow of signalling molecules and electrical signals
- Common lipids in membranes:![[Pasted image 20260424102331.png]]
	- Storage lipids: Triacylglycerols
	- Membrane lipids:
		- Phospholipids
		- Glycolipids
		- Sterols
- Sterols consist of four fused carbon rings
	- Has high rigidity and almost planar
	- Precursors to steroid hormones

##### Membrane transport
Membrane lipids:
- Amphipathic: hydrophilic and hydrophobic moieties

Lipids cluster together in water
- Glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids, and sterols:
	- Virtually insoluble in water
	- Spontaneously form microscopic lipid aggregates when mixed with water: micelles and bilayers turning into vesicles
	- Reduces the hydrophobic surface exposed to water
	- Vesicles can have an aqueous interior
- Biological membranes are lipid bilayers
- Membranes are bound intermolecularly, and the fluid mosaic model describes the mosaic of components that make up a membrane.
- Membranes contain besides the lipid bilayer: proteins, sterols, glycoproteins, etc.

Single/multi-pass transmembrane helices:
- Multipass:
	- Cross the membrane several times
	- Have multiple hydrophobic sequences of ~20 residues that each cross the membrane when in the alpha-helical conformation

Dynamics:
- Membranes have high lateral mobility but bad "flip-flop" diffusion
- Membrane proteins facilitate translocation of individual lipids![[Pasted image 20260424110329.png|440]]
- The names are real
#### Membrane transport
- Cells must take up and retain materials from surroundings
- Must also release by-products
- Restrict ions/metabolites to certain organelles
- Control of what goes in and out is crucial!
	- Nonpolar compounds can dissolve in the lipid bilayer and cross the membrane unassisted
	- Charged or polar solutes are generally unable to cross the membrane
	- Electrochemical gradient determines the direction in which charged solutes moves.
	- Gradients are utilized in biochemical processes via membrane transporters and ion channels

Transporters:
- Ion channels transport down the gradient and may be gated by a ligand or ion
- Primary active transport, goes against electrochemical gradient. Uses ATP
	- P-type ATPases: animal cell antiporter for Na+ and K+
	- Integral in keeping charge and potential separation between inside and outside the membrane
	- 3 Na+ flows out and 2 K+ flows in, more positive thrown out and inside become net negative
- Secondary active transport: uses gradient created by primary active transport to drive biochemical process
	- Na+ glucose symporter: takes up glucose from intestine driven by downhill flow of Na+

Viral fusion proteins (membranes as barriers vs. parasites)
- Enable viruses to enter host cells
- Virus membrane fuses with host cell membrane and spills genetic material into host cell
